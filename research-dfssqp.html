Current research project

Derivative-free optimization for constrained learning

Applying derivative-free stochastic SQP to a learning problem where every objective evaluation requires model training.

With Prof. Sen NaGeorgia Tech ISyEWork in progress

The question

How can an optimization algorithm make progress when its objective is expensive to evaluate, its feedback is noisy, and the decision variable must satisfy a constraint?

This project studies an application of derivative-free stochastic sequential quadratic programming (DF-SSQP) to single-index learning. The algorithm selects a direction w that compresses 48 features into a scalar index. For each candidate direction, a neural model is trained and its development loss is evaluated. Training randomness makes that loss stochastic.

The experiments use a financial forecasting application as a test setting. The central research question concerns the behavior of the optimization method: how it improves the learning objective while satisfying the equality constraint.

My work: formulating the learning problem, implementing and verifying the paper’s algorithm for this application, and designing experiments to evaluate its behavior under different sources of randomness and constraint information.

The approach

The single-index direction satisfies a unit-norm equality constraint. Each iteration uses two perturbed directions to estimate an objective gradient, then solves an SQP system to update the direction.

  1. Evaluate a pair of directions

    Train a fresh model at each perturbed direction. Both fits share the same initialization and minibatch randomness, making their development losses a paired comparison.

  2. Estimate derivatives and compute a step

    Average the objective-gradient estimates over iterations. Compare an analytic constraint Jacobian (AI) with a zeroth-order estimate (ZI), using the first-order variant with an identity Hessian model.

  3. Track progress across stochastic runs

    Start all runs from the same direction and track development MSE and constraint violation as the number of SQP steps increases.

Model and optimization problem

The prediction model combines a control variable um with a neural function of the single index w⊤zm:

ŷm(w, ξ) = β(w, ξ)um + Fθ(w, ξ)(w⊤zm)

Here, β and θ are the outputs of a fixed stochastic training procedure. The outer objective is expected development mean squared error:

minw ∈ ℝ⁴⁸ Eξ[L(w, ξ)]   subject to   w⊤w − 1 = 0.

The expectation is over training randomness ξ, with the data and training procedure fixed. AI and ZI both estimate objective gradients from function values; they differ in their constraint-Jacobian information.

Research direction

The broader goal is to understand how DF-SSQP can be applied effectively to constrained learning problems where objective evaluations involve stochastic model training. This project connects the algorithm’s formulation with an implementation and empirical study in a real-data learning application.

Using the single-index model as a concrete setting, I aim to identify what makes the method effective in practice and what its limitations are. The emphasis is on understanding the optimization method and developing insights that can inform its use in other learning problems.
